A guide for brothers and sisters

The Food Alarm

So your brother has something called ARFID. You've probably thought at least once: why can't he just eat the food?

Fair question. This explains it. There are a couple of games. It takes about ten minutes.

Level 1 · What it even is

ARFID is a real thing with a real name

AAvoidant
RRestrictive
FFood
IIntake
DDisorder

Translation: your brother's brain makes eating a lot of foods feel hard, gross, or straight-up scary. So he avoids them and sticks to a small list of foods that feel safe.

Doctors treat it. It has nothing to do with wanting to be skinny, and it is not a phase or a bad attitude.

Level 2 · Try it yourself

The worm test

🪱🍽️

Picture this. Someone puts a plate of cold, wiggly worms in front of you and says, "Come on, it's just one bite." Your stomach flips. Your throat gets tight. You are not hungry anymore, even if you were starving a minute ago.

That feeling is your brain's alarm. Everybody has one. In your brother, the alarm goes off for foods that seem totally normal to you. Pasta. A new brand of chicken nuggets. Something that touched the sauce.

Now press the buttons and watch what different stuff at the dinner table does to the alarm.

Uneasy 70/100

Start: worms on the plate. Alarm at 70.

The big idea: when the alarm is loud, a body basically can't eat. Hunger shuts off. That's why yelling, bribing, or daring doesn't work. It makes the alarm louder.

Level 6 · The part about you

Real talk: this can be annoying

Plans change because of food. Your brother might get extra attention. Dinner can be tense. And now somebody handed you a guide about being careful with your words.

Feeling annoyed, left out, or frustrated about that is allowed. It doesn't make you a bad brother.

  • Take those feelings to a parent, privately. Not to the dinner table.
  • Ask for one-on-one time if you're not getting enough. That's a fair thing to want.
  • You are not the food police. Getting your brother to eat is not your job. That's for his doctors, his therapist, and your parents.
  • Your job is way easier: be a normal, decent brother at the table.
